SuperInstance is a distributed computing ecosystem built on a ternary design principle: every signal is classified as negative, zero, or positive {-1, 0, +1}, and every operation conserves the total ternary charge. This isn't arbitrary — it's the same conservation law that appears in Z₃ lattice gauge theory, where Gauss's law ensures charge is neither created nor destroyed. We apply this principle to fleet routing (bottles), agent coordination (cycles), and space traversal (depth), not as metaphor but as engineering constraint: one bottle in, one bottle out; one cycle logged, one quality signal emitted; one room entered, one room exited. The math is real. 1,596 repos. 660+ automated builds. The conservation law is the invariant that makes the whole system auditable.

The principle

γ + η = C

Three axioms. (1) Every signal in the system is classified into one of three states: negative, zero, or positive — the elements of Z₃. (2) Every operation conserves total ternary charge. One bottle in, one bottle out. The sum of trits across a boundary is invariant. (3) This conservation law is structurally isomorphic to Gauss's law in Z₃ lattice gauge theory — the total ternary flux through any closed surface vanishes.

This is not a metaphor. It is an engineering constraint enforced at six layers: definition, runtime, build-time lint, compile-time injection, GPU kernel boundaries, and CDN edge. The fleet, agent, and space subsystems are gauge-coupled sectors that inherit this conservation law by construction.
